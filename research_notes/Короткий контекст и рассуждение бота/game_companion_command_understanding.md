## 1. Context-sensitive commands in shipped squad games: how one generic input becomes a specific order

### Takeaway
Shipped squad shooters do not interpret meaning; they resolve one order input against (a) what the crosshair is on (object type selects the verb, ground point supplies the place) and (b) a small set of standing squad modes that persist until replaced. Public documentation of the exact resolution rules is thin: most of it is manuals, previews and reviews, and I found no developer postmortem that spells out a full "default order" table.

### Cited Findings

#### Star Wars: Republic Commando (2005)
- The squad can be ordered to "move to secure any position (wherever the crosshair is pointed)", plus search-and-destroy and group-up / spread-out orders. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Republic_Commando)
- Objects highlight under the crosshair and "the player can then press the 'use' key to issue an order automatically associated with the object": a sealed door gives a synchronised breach-and-clear, a console gives "slice", cargo boxes with a vantage give a Sniper / Anti-Armor position. So object type selects the verb, and one key issues it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Republic_Commando); same mechanism described in [VideoGameGeek](https://videogamegeek.com/videogame/74069/star-wars-republic-commando)
- The pending order is shown before it is issued: a holographic context-sensitive order appears on the visor and one button assigns squad members to it (press review). — [FutureFive](https://futurefive.co.nz/story/star-wars-republic-commando)
- Who does it is resolved by the game, not the player: "Where possible, the squadmates will usually take their preferred roles (sniper, demolitions and technical)." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Republic_Commando)
- When the player is downed they can order squad mates to revive them or to keep their current orders, so standing orders survive the player's incapacitation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Republic_Commando)
- The squad AI programmer (John Hancock) gave every speech situation a unique cue ID such as "KilledDroid" or "AssistingLeader", starting from a list of about 50 cues that included responses to player commands (developer diary, read through a search summary; the page itself returned HTTP 403). — [GameSpot designer diary #4](https://www.gamespot.com/pc/action/starwarsrepubliccommando/preview_6117992.html)
- Steam store text "the simple touch of one button easily controls your squad to perform complex commands" is marketing wording, not a description of rules. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/6000)

#### Brothers in Arms: Road to Hill 30 (2005)
- Gearbox describes a chain-of-command design: the player gives intent to fire-team leaders, not to individuals. Looking at a firing position and pressing the command button makes the team move there, take cover and fire. Pointing at an enemy position and pressing the command button sets the target and suppresses it (developer Q&A, read through a search summary). — [GameSpot Q&A](https://gamespot.com/articles/brothers-in-arms-road-to-hill-30-qanda-final-thoughts/1100-6119621/)
- The result of an order is shown as world-space state: suppression indicator circles over enemies go from red to grey as covering fire takes effect; the indicators can be switched off. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_in_Arms:_Road_to_Hill_30); [Worthplaying review](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/4/7/reviews/23699-pc-review-brothers-in-arms-road-to-hill-30/)

#### Full Spectrum Warrior (2004)
- The command cursor is context-sensitive and shows the changed order before it is given: moving the cursor to a corner changes the order from wall stacking to corner stacking. A fire team covers a sector when the player selects it and points in a direction; the soldiers shift on their own to get a clear shot (press preview). — [GameSpot impressions](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/full-spectrum-warrior-impressions/1100-6028426/)
- A command is not executed instantly: the team leader first receives it by radio and relays it, which doubles as audible confirmation (press review, attribution among the reviews found is not exact). — [GamesFirst](https://gamesfirst.com/articles/2005/full-spectrum-warrior/)

#### SWAT 4 (2005)
- Two command modes exist: Classic (SWAT 3 style number-key hierarchy) and Graphic (a menu on right-click). — [StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/SWAT_4/Controls)
- The developers designed the Graphic Command Interface to keep SWAT 3's range of orders but issue them with the second mouse button instead of several key presses (developer interview, read through a search summary). — [TweakTown interview](https://www.tweaktown.com/gaming/2244/swat_4_pc_interview/index.html)
- "Smart commands": the game guesses the order the player most likely wants from the selected element and what the crosshair is on, defaults to it, and the player can still override through the menu (player write-up). — [Quarter to Three forum](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/swat-4/17875)
- Examples of the target selecting the verb: a door offers "Open and Clear" / "Open, Bang, and Clear"; a locked door offers pick lock or breach; a civilian offers restrain; the cursor icon itself changes with the spot on the door it rests on. — [GameSpot review](https://gamespot.com/reviews/swat-4-review/1900-6121423/); [GameSpot final hands-on](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/swat-4-final-hands-on/1100-6121046)
- The team is addressed as elements (red, blue, gold = all). The expansion The Stetchkov Syndicate added holding a command until the leader gives the signal. Officers "will often, but not always" wait for the player before acting. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAT_4)

#### Rainbow Six 3 (Xbox, 2003) and Rainbow Six: Vegas (2006)
- Rainbow Six 3 on console removed planning and compensated with better squad autonomy: team mates automatically duck behind objects or take covering positions in an area. Orders go through a menu or through voice on the Xbox Live headset. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy%27s_Rainbow_Six_3:_Raven_Shield)
- Vegas: ordering team mates to open doors, defuse bombs or pick targets takes one button press; doors bring up a context-sensitive iconic menu of entry options. — [Empire review](https://www.empireonline.com/gaming/reviews/tom-clancys-rainbow-six-vegas-review); [GameSpot E3 2006](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/e3-06-tom-clancys-rainbow-six-vegas/1100-6150711/)
- Vegas keeps a persistent squad behaviour mode (assault versus infiltration) that is toggled with one button, separate from the point orders. — [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/reviews/tom-clancy-s-rainbow-six-vegas-xbox-360)

#### Ghost Recon: Advanced Warfighter (2006) and Ghost Recon Breakpoint (AI team mates added July 2020)
- Advanced Warfighter's Cross-Com gives orders to AI team mates and support assets; on consoles orders apply to the whole squad, on PC to individuals. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy%27s_Ghost_Recon_Advanced_Warfighter)
- Breakpoint's order set is tiny and mode-like: "Cleared Hot" (open fire on hostiles nearby), "Regroup", "Go To" (a location), plus Hold Position, which sends team mates to the closest cover where they stay. Sync shots are issued by aiming at a target and pressing a prompt. — [Xbox Wire / Ubisoft](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2020/07/12/ghost-recon-breakpoint-ai-teammates-update/); [MP1st patch notes](https://mp1st.com/?p=128516)

#### Half-Life 2 (2004)
- Citizens join the player's squad when they see him; a designer flag lets a citizen stay in the squad but speak a line instead of obeying a move order. — [Valve Developer Wiki](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Npc_citizen)

### Inferences
- The common resolution rule is: crosshair hit → object class → default verb; if the hit is plain ground, the default verb is "move / secure here"; if it is an enemy, "attack / suppress this". Applied to the project, a pin or a crosshair hit is a fully valid place argument for any verb that lacks one, so "Shoot, shoot, shoot" with an active pin should resolve to "open fire at the pin" in the same way Brothers in Arms resolves "command button while looking at an enemy position".
- None of these games asks the player to name the place in the order. Place always comes from pointing. A voice order that names no place is therefore the normal case, not an under-specified one, and the resolver, not the classifier, should supply the place.
- Orders come in two kinds that the games keep apart: point orders (go there, breach this) and standing modes (assault / infiltrate, cleared hot, hold). A new point order replaces the previous point order but leaves the modes alone. This matches the project's "standing orders" and suggests a correction such as "No, here" should replace only the place of the last point order.
- Several games show the resolved order before or while it is carried out (visor hologram, cursor change, icon). A voice bot has no cursor, so the equivalent is a spoken echo that contains the resolved verb and place.
- Assigning roles by the game (Republic Commando) and letting team mates pick cover themselves (Rainbow Six 3 console, Breakpoint Hold Position) is how designers remove the need for the player to specify details.

### Gaps
- No developer talk or postmortem was found that documents the default-order table of Republic Commando, SWAT 4, Rainbow Six or Ghost Recon. The rules above are reconstructed from manuals, reviews and one developer Q&A.
- Not researched because the search budget ran out: Mass Effect (power wheel and move/attack d-pad), Gears of War, Arma 3, Squad, Door Kickers, Ghost Recon Wildlands, Ready or Not's context menu, SOCOM's menu grammar, Rainbow Six Siege's older single-player modes. Wikipedia's Ready or Not page only confirms context menus and red / blue / gold elements. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_or_Not_(video_game))
- How long an order persists and what the squad does when it ends is documented only anecdotally. One non-authoritative 2026 guide claims Half-Life 2 rebels hold a commanded position for fifteen seconds and then return; not verified. — [fan guide](https://gamertagmythras.com/blog/half-life-2/half-life-2-uprising-guide)
- The Last of Us has no direct command input for Ellie, so it belongs to section 4.

## 2. Voice-commanded games: grammars, misrecognition, and what reviews say worked

### Takeaway
Every shipped voice-command game before the language-model era used a small fixed grammar of the form who-what-where with named waypoints, chose words for acoustic distinctness, showed the valid words on screen, and kept a button fallback. Reviews praise the ones with narrow grammars and a detached commander view (Tom Clancy's EndWar, There Came an Echo) and punish free or fuzzy recognition during action (Binary Domain, Lifeline, Bot Colony).

### Cited Findings

#### Grammar
- A survey of 449 video games and 22 audio games with voice input (CHI PLAY 2018) names the dominant command pattern "Who-What-Where Commands": "an imperative phrase that directs a subject (who) to take an action (what) in a place (where), in that order. The 'who' or 'what' element is sometimes unstated when the target subject or place is implicit in the rest of the command phrase or due to the context." — [Allison, Carter, Gibbs, Smith 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)
- The same paper explains why places are named waypoints: voice lacks "a precise locus of manipulation, like a mouse pointer", and unique names give "an unambiguous and stable link between a game object and a specific utterance". Waypoints "reduce the decision space" at the price of restricting where the player can send units. — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)
- There Came an Echo examples: "Miranda move to Alpha Six", "Everybody focus fire on Target Two on my mark". The second adds a "when" slot: the order stays inactive until the player says "mark". — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)
- The paper separates "Name an Action" (a discrete act) from "Name a Behaviour" (a mode such as FIFA 14's "ultra-defensive"), noting that behaviour commands move decisions to the AI and give "a more mediated sense of control". — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)
- The paper's conclusion: the field is "overly concerned with ... word recognition accuracy rates"; patterns such as Waypoints and Spellcasting "constrain speech input to clear, easily distinguishable phrases that allow imperfect speech recognition systems to recognise utterances with a high degree of accuracy". — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)

#### Tom Clancy's EndWar (2008)
- Units are numbered, directives are short phrases, and map objectives carry call signs; all commands are built from those three parts (player review). A developer sample is "Red unit, capture strategic point delta". — [Giant Bomb user review](https://www.giantbomb.com/tom-clancys-endwar/3030-20739/user-reviews/2200-7323/); [Shacknews first look](https://www.shacknews.com/article/47087/tom-clancys-endwar-first-look)
- Holding the trigger shows the list of commands that apply to the current situation; saying a word opens a sub-list. The options are context-sensitive to the selected units. The system is described as based on SOCOM's. — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/47087/tom-clancys-endwar-first-look); [HEXUS](https://hexus.net/gaming/features/xbox-360/8838-tom-clancys-endwar-pc-xbox-360-ps3/?page=2)
- Word choice was tuned for recognition: Julian Gerighty said replacing "capture" with "secure" improved recognition by about five percent, claimed 95 to 97 percent of commands recognised in all languages, and said players cannot add commands (developer claims, not independently measured; read through a search summary because the page returned HTTP 502). — [bit-tech interview](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/xbox/tom-clancy-s-end-war-interview/1/)
- Playtesters who got used to speaking used voice more than the pad even though "move to reticule" was quicker with one button. — [HEXUS](https://hexus.net/gaming/features/xbox-360/8838-tom-clancys-endwar-pc-xbox-360-ps3/?page=2)
- Reception: The Guardian noted an "occasional voice recognition blip"; GameSpot praised the "Innovative voice command mechanic". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy%27s_EndWar)
- The CHI PLAY paper classes EndWar as "Floating Commander" and argues the detached view lets the player give full attention to commanding, which is why complex who-what-where grammars suit strategy games. — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)

#### Shooters where the player also fights ("Situated Commander")
- The paper warns that commanding by voice while controlling an avatar "requires the player to divide their attention ... as speech formulation draws on some of the same cognitive resources that are needed for strategic problem solving, to a greater extent than hand-eye coordination does". Its example is Rainbow Six: Vegas with "check your fire" and "regroup on me". — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)
- SOCOM: U.S. Navy SEALs (2002) shipped with a Logitech USB headset and was the first PlayStation 2 title in North America with voice recognition; orders could be given by headset or through the on-screen Team Command Menu (one review mentions some extra speech-only commands). — [Sony press release](https://sony.mediaroom.com/2002-05-20-SOCOM-U.S.-Navy-SEALs-Allows-Players-to-Command-Their-Teammates-Through-Revolutionary-Voice-Recognition-and-Online-Communication-Technology-For-Playstation-2); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCOM_U.S._Navy_SEALs_(video_game))
- Rainbow Six 3 (Xbox): recognition confused acoustically close orders, "slight misunderstandings between similar phrases" such as "open frag and clear" and "open flash and clear". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy%27s_Rainbow_Six_3:_Raven_Shield)
- Rainbow Six: Vegas: the squad can be directed with the D-pad or with a headset on Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3. — [Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/tom-clancys-rainbow-six-vegas/61-3550/)
- Mass Effect 3 with Kinect (2012): previews found it worked when combined with the pad, with a slight lag before a command registered, occasional failures in a noisy room, and wording that reminded the player they were talking to a game. Reviewers valued that the controller stays in the hands as fallback. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2012-01-18-testing-kinect-in-mass-effect-3-plus-demo-coming-february-14.html); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/mass_effect_3/b/xbox360/archive/2012/01/18/mass-effect-3-preview-not-bad-with-kinect); [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/3054-kinect-effect-commanding-shepards-squad-with-voice)
- Binary Domain (2012): commands by button or by voice in six languages, with trust levels changed by performance and by conversation. PC Gamer reported the system "routinely misunderstands or ignores commands" and registers commands when nothing was said, in a quiet room with a headset; another reviewer switched to buttons; Computer and Video Games called recognition "largely terrible". One positive review advised keeping common orders on buttons. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_Domain); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/binary-domain-review); [Gaming Pastime](https://gamingpastime.com/binary-domain-pc-review/); [CheatCC](https://cheatcc.com/articles/binary-domain-review-for-xbox-360-xbox360/)

#### Voice-only designs
- There Came an Echo (2015): "command aliasing" lets the player enter a custom word or phrase for every unit and command; reviewers found recognition good but noted that units can only go to pre-set locations, cover is automatic by proximity, and there is a delay between saying an order and its execution. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2014/04/10/there-came-an-echo-voice-controlled-strategy-on-ps4/comment-page-1); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/came-echo-review-dont-yell)
- Lifeline / Operator's Side (2003): about 500 commands and a headline capacity of 5,000 words and 100,000 phrases; "widely panned upon release due to the gimmicky and imprecise use of voice commands". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeline_(video_game)); [PSX Data Center](https://psxdatacenter.com/psx2/games2/SLUS-20848.html)
- Bot Colony (alpha 2013, cancelled January 2015, returned 2017): free natural-language speech to robots, speech-to-text on the client, language understanding on a server farm. Rock Paper Shotgun: "When [Bot Colony] works, it's oddly satisfying. When it doesn't work, it's generally amusing." It sold 814 units before cancellation (the Wikipedia page carries a conflict-of-interest banner). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bot_Colony)
- In Verbis Virtus uses invented spell words ("ekto lumeh", "obee kehnu"), which the pattern paper cites as a way to keep phrases acoustically distinct. — [Allison et al. 2018](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/ec0a989c-f586-5d01-b847-8f10fbd3cde8/content)

### Inferences
- The games that reviewed well made the "where" slot unnecessary to say correctly (named waypoints) or unnecessary to say at all (pointing). The project already has pointing and pins, which is a stronger "where" channel than any of these games had. The lesson is to stop expecting the utterance to carry the place.
- The who-what-where definition explicitly allows unstated slots resolved "due to the context". Slot defaults are part of the established pattern, not an extension of it.
- A "when" slot ("on my mark", "when I say so", SWAT 4's hold-until-signal) is a cheap and well-liked addition. The project's "wait" / "go" intents already form this pair; binding "go" to the most recent held order is the same mechanism.
- Acoustically close orders are a recurring failure (frag / flash). With 41 intents, confusable pairs should be found from the classifier's confusion matrix and separated by wording in the suggested phrase list rather than by more training alone.
- The attention cost of speaking while fighting argues for very short utterances and for the bot filling the gaps, which is the owner's request stated as a human-factors finding.

### Gaps
- No postmortem with grammar sizes was found for SOCOM, Rainbow Six 3 or Vegas, Binary Domain or Mass Effect 3. EndWar's vocabulary size and speech vendor were not found.
- Not researched (search budget): Arma 3 voice mods, VoiceAttack setups, Radio Commander (the store page describes radio orders, and nothing found says it accepts speech — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/871530/Radio_Commander/)), Ready or Not's speech feature.
- The paper "'I Didn't Catch That, But I'll Try My Best': Anticipatory Error Handling in a Voice Controlled Game" (Zargham, Pfau, Schnackenberg, Malaka, CHI 2022) studies a game that guesses the intended command instead of asking the player to repeat. Only its existence and setup (a voice-commanded herding dog) were confirmed; the results were not retrieved (ACM returned HTTP 403). — [University of Bremen](https://www.uni-bremen.de/en/dmlab/news/details/presentations-on-chi-2022-in-new-orleans)
- No quantitative usability study comparing echo confirmation styles for voice squad commands was found.

## 3. Architectures that give a character light reasoning at low cost

### Takeaway
The documented systems all get "reasoning" from a small symbolic world state plus priority or scoring, not from search depth: F.E.A.R. plans are usually one or two actions long, Killzone 2 replans every bot at 5 Hz, Halo and Valve use prioritised lists and most-specific-rule-wins. Each has a documented mechanism for memory and for predictability, and each could fill a missing order slot from state.

### Cited Findings

#### Goal-Oriented Action Planning (F.E.A.R., Jeff Orkin, GDC 2006)
- The state machine has three states (Goto, Animate, UseSmartObject); the logic for when to switch lives in a planner. Goals compete by priority and the planner tries to satisfy the highest one; a character with no valid plan for a goal falls back to the next goal. — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- World state is "an array of four-byte values" (for example TargetDead, WeaponLoaded, AtNode); preconditions and effects use the same fixed array, so chaining is trivial: "the Attack action has the precondition that our weapon is loaded, and the Reload action has the effect that the weapon is loaded." — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Because the array has one slot per concept, the character "can only reason about one of each during planning"; "attention-selection sub-systems outside of the planner" choose the current weapon and target. — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Cost control: a cost per action steers A*; expensive checks such as "is there a path to safety" run as procedural preconditions "on-demand only when necessary". — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Failure memory drives replanning: a soldier whose door is blocked records it in working memory, replans to kick the door, then to dive through the window. — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Orders are goals, not overrides: "A.I. have goals to respond to orders, and it is up to the A.I. to prioritize following those orders versus satisfying other goals. For example, fleeing from danger may trump following an order to advance." — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- There were four simple squad behaviours (Get-to-Cover, Advance-Cover, Orderly-Advance, Search) and no complex ones: "we actually did not have any complex squad behaviors at all in F.E.A.R."; apparent flanking is "a side effect of moving to the only available valid cover". — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Size and depth, from a later analysis of the game files: just under 70 goals, 120 actions, plans typically one or two actions long with a few of three or four. — [Thompson, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-the-ai-of-f-e-a-r-with-goal-oriented-action-planning)
- Orkin's closing section proposes treating dialogue lines as plan actions with preconditions and effects, and plan recognition to "understand ambiguous speech from the player, such as 'Can you help me this?'" by guessing which plan the player is following (prototype by Gorniak and Roy, 2005, not a shipped game). — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)

#### Hierarchical Task Networks (Killzone 2, Transformers: Fall of Cybertron)
- Killzone 2 multiplayer bots: three layers (strategy, squad, individual). Orders flow down (Defend, Advance, MoveTo), reports flow up ("Order failed", combat information, requests for reassignment). — [Guerrilla Games slides, 2009](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots)
- Domain size and cost: 360 methods, 1,048 branches, 138 behaviours, 147 "continue" branches; replanning at 5 Hz; with 14 bots plus turrets and drones about 500 plans per second, about 8,000 decompositions per second (15 per plan on average) and about 24,000 branch evaluations per second. — [Guerrilla Games slides, 2009](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots)
- Predictability device: "continue" branches keep the current plan during replanning to prevent twitchy behaviour; plans can remember and forget facts, for example to keep attacking the same target. — [Guerrilla Games slides, 2009](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots)
- Transformers: Fall of Cybertron (Troy Humphreys, Game AI Pro): world state is "a vector of properties" indexed by an enum; compound tasks have ordered methods with conditions and the planner takes "the first set of conditions that are valid", so priority is authored by order. — [Game AI Pro, chapter 12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)
- Replanning has three triggers: "the NPC finishes or fails the current plan, the NPC does not have a plan, or the NPC's world state changes via a sensor." A running plan is replaced only by a higher-priority one, tracked with a method traversal record. — [Game AI Pro, chapter 12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)
- Cost: depth-first, no heuristic and no sorting; these features "allowed the HTN planner in Transformers: Fall of Cybertron to be considerably faster than our GOAP system used in Transformers: War for Cybertron". Partial plans (plan only a few steps) were authored by hand into the domains. — [Game AI Pro, chapter 12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)

#### Behaviour trees with orders and styles (Halo 2, Halo 3)
- Halo 2's core behaviour graph has "on the order of 50 different behaviors"; relevance tests are binary, and the prioritised list is "by far the most commonly used" decision scheme, because tuning floating-point weights across twenty or more choices becomes "almost impossible". — [Isla, GDC 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Stimulus behaviours: an event handler adds a behaviour to the tree for "one or two seconds", so an event is considered in context and higher-priority behaviours still get their say. — [Isla, GDC 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Orders and styles: an order gives a squad a set of positions and flags (vehicle use, stealth, rules of engagement) and references a style; a style is a list of allowed and disallowed behaviours (defensive forbids charge and search; aggressive forbids self-preservation). Designers pick from a small approved library of styles instead of editing them per encounter. — [Isla, GDC 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Stated principle: "Value explicitness above all other things", avoid dithering, and aim for an observer being able to guess the AI's internal state. The AI "has to run at 30Hz or more". — [Isla, GDC 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- Halo 3 objectives: a tree of prioritised tasks, each self-describing with priority, activation condition and capacity; squads fall to the highest-priority open task and are pushed back up when a higher task opens. — [Isla, GDC 2008 slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/combat-evolved-the-encounter-design-of-halo-3)

#### Utility AI (The Sims, Guild Wars 2: Heart of Thorns)
- The Sims (2000) combined a character's current need with a score offered by an object or activity that can satisfy it; this is described as one of the first visible uses of utility AI and the core mechanic of the game. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_system)
- Heart of Thorns uses Dave Mark's Infinite Axis Utility System: each possible action is a decision score evaluator; each consideration is an input normalised to [0, 1], passed through a response curve, and the results "are then multiplied together". — [Lewis, Game AI Pro 3, chapter 13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Cost control: "any consideration can disqualify an entire decision from being selected simply by scoring zero", the evaluator stops early, and cheap considerations are ordered before expensive ones such as a line-of-sight raycast. — [Lewis, Game AI Pro 3](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Predictability: most curves come "from a small palette of preset curves"; oscillation between close scores is fought with a "commitment bonus" for the last choice, decision weights, and run-time and cooldown considerations; a sequence is made by cooldowns plus slightly decreasing weights. — [Lewis, Game AI Pro 3](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Advice on inputs: prefer a simple proxy variable (a time constant) over an exact model, because "Players will, in general, be eager to add their own narrative reasons for why AI does what it does." — [Lewis, Game AI Pro 3](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Dave Mark's site describes the system as entirely data-driven and claims designers built NPC packages "in as little as 7 minutes" (vendor claim). — [gameai.com](https://gameai.com/iaus.php)

#### Stimulus-response rule databases (Valve, Elan Ruskin, GDC 2012)
- The session abstract: the system scores hundreds of world facts in a fuzzy pattern match against thousands of candidate lines, lets characters remember history, and goes from special cases to general ones; writers add cases without programmers. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015528/AI-driven-Dynamic-Dialog-through)
- It applies "the most specific one it can find, using less-specific ones as fall-backs", and is built so that exchanges break off when a character comes under attack. The characters are not planning; the system creates "an illusion of situational awareness". — [Emily Short's write-up](https://emshort.blog/2012/03/16/gdc-2012-talk-on-dynamic-dialogue/)
- The Source engine form: a speaker raises a concept with criteria; the system searches rules for one matching the concept and criteria and plays one of its responses (read through a search summary; the page returned HTTP 403). — [Valve Developer Wiki](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Response_System)
- A practitioner's summary of the same talk: write many events each with one to many conditions; the most specific that fits wins, otherwise a general one, a "self-branching structure"; he judged about 1,000 rules workable without Ruskin's optimisations. — [Radiator blog](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2012/07/rule-databases-for-contextual-narrative.html)

#### Smart objects and layered commander / squad AI
- F.E.A.R.'s UseSmartObject state takes its animation from an object in the game database, and designers fill spaces with opportunities (cover, windows, flanking entries) instead of scripting individuals. — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Killzone 2's designers follow "Create the information by hand first, then automate it later if necessary" for level annotations, and the strategy layer separates what to do from how. — [Guerrilla Games slides, 2009](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots)

### Inferences
- For turning an under-specified order into a specific one, the cheapest fit is the Valve pattern: treat the classifier output plus context as a query of facts (intent, place named or not, pin active and its age, crosshair target class, last order intent, last order place, last order outcome, seconds since last order, last bark, ammo state) and keep a rule table sorted by specificity. "Shoot" + pin active + no place named → fire at pin. "Shoot" + no pin + crosshair on enemy → fire at that enemy. "Shoot" alone → fire at current target (general fallback). The cost is a few dozen comparisons per utterance.
- "No, here, on my pin" is the same query with LastOrder facts: negation + a deictic place + a recent last order → re-issue the last intent with the new place and cancel the wrong action. "Shoot again" → re-issue the last fire order unchanged. Both need only a one-order memory with a time stamp, like Halo 2's short-lived stimulus behaviours.
- "Go find ammo" is a two-step plan at F.E.A.R.'s typical depth: Attack has precondition WeaponLoaded; HasAmmo is false; the action that produces it is PickUp(ammo source), which needs Goto. A GOAP or small HTN with a dozen world-state symbols covers this class of request (find ammo, get health, revive, come back) without new intents per item.
- Orkin's fixed one-slot-per-concept world state with attention selection outside the planner is the same shape as the project's single {intent, place, direction} order: the resolver picks the one place in focus, the planner reasons only about that.
- Predictability comes from four repeatable devices: binary relevance and prioritised lists rather than tuned floats (Halo 2), ordered methods (HTN), a commitment bonus or "continue" branch for the current choice (Guild Wars 2, Killzone 2), and a small approved library of styles (Halo 2). A deterministic planner that already exists in the project can adopt all four.
- Orders as goals that compete with self-preservation (F.E.A.R.) and as a branch below a survival reflex (see PUBG Ally in section 5) are the same design in two generations. It also gives a principled place for the bot to refuse or defer and say why.

### Gaps
- Ruskin's slides and the Valve wiki page could not be opened (host not found, HTTP 403). The details usually quoted from the talk (key-value facts, counting matched criteria, responses that write facts back with an expiry, a line triggering another character's follow-up) are therefore not cited here from a primary source and should be verified before being stated as fact.
- Dave Mark's 2013 and 2015 GDC talks are behind the GDC Vault paywall; the mechanics are cited from Mike Lewis's chapter instead.
- Horizon Zero Dawn's HTN, Killzone 3 and Company of Heroes squad AI were not researched (search budget).
- No published per-frame millisecond budgets were found for any of these systems; only Killzone 2's plan counts and Halo 2's 30 Hz requirement.

## 4. Companion design lessons: what makes a companion seem to understand

### Takeaway
The published companion talks agree that the impression of understanding comes from restraint and readable timing rather than from intelligence: never ruin what the player is doing, act on the player's implied intent (fire only when the player has gone loud), keep proactive help rare, say only things the player can verify, and use speech to explain actions and inaction.

### Cited Findings

#### Ellie, The Last of Us (Max Dyckhoff, Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35; GDC 2014)
- Goal: "We had to make sure that Ellie never annoyed or frustrated the player". An early demo had buddies "positioning themselves awkwardly, shooting at the wrong time, and generally getting underfoot". — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Fire permission is inferred from the player's behaviour: "instead of being happy to shoot most of the time ... Ellie wants to not shoot. ... We then built logic to give her permission to fire. If the player is actively firing a weapon or engaging in noisy melee combat, then Ellie is allowed to fire upon her target." She may also fire if the player is in immediate danger, and loses permission when the player is judged to have returned to stealth. — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Anticipation: the brick throw is triggered by "hooking into the enemy perception and movement systems and predicting if they will be able to see the player in the near future". — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Proactive help sits "behind really big timers": in a playtest she gave a stuck player ammo every minute, which "devalued the act of gifting". — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Callouts must be verifiable: "it is absolutely imperative to ensure that whenever a buddy calls out an enemy, the enemy is there for the player to see"; a callout the player cannot confirm "reflected badly on Ellie's intelligence". The callout system models what the player is unaware of. — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Speech instead of risky action: rather than running out of hiding when about to be seen, "she can vocalize the threat and allow the player to handle it". — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Final rule: "your buddy AI can be as intelligent or realistic as possible, but if they ruin what the player is trying to do just once, it's all in vain." Most development time went into iteration on follow positions, firing parameters and dialogue triggers, not new systems. — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Follow positions are rated by distance to threats and allies, angle to the player and whether geometry blocks her view of Joel; cover points come from about 80 raycasts around the player. The companion system was rebuilt roughly five months before release. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/endure-and-survive-the-ai-of-the-last-of-us)

#### Elizabeth, BioShock Infinite (John Abercrombie, GDC 2014, through a press write-up)
- She is kept between the player and the destination ("goal-side", borrowed from football defending) so that her actions are seen; if the player gets far ahead she is teleported quietly, on the principle that players will not know. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-soccer-and-theater-helped-irrational-create-bioshock-infinites-elizabeth/)
- Her spoken enemy callouts were often missed, so the team added an on-screen spotlight icon. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-soccer-and-theater-helped-irrational-create-bioshock-infinites-elizabeth/)

#### Atreus, God of War (Hayato Yoshidome, GDC 2019)
- The talk covers the archetypes tried and "pitfalls to each iteration" in making Atreus a companion without diluting Kratos's combat; a PlayStation video reported the aim that he help "without too much micromanagement" and not be a burden. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025768/Raising-Atreus-for-Battle-in); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/news/god-of-wars-developers-delve-into-atreus-abilities-in-new-video/)

#### Survivor bots, Left 4 Dead (Michael Booth, 2009)
- Bots must be believed "fair": imperfect knowledge through simulated senses, simulated aiming, reaction times, and "Reliable and predictable decision making". — [Booth, Valve slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf) (read through a [text mirror](https://www.readkong.com/page/the-ai-systems-of-left-4-dead-michael-booth-valve-9664541))
- Hard guarantees remove the worst outcomes: "SurvivorBots cannot deal friendly fire damage", "SurvivorBots never use Molotovs", they prioritise human team mates over bots, and a stray bot is teleported near the team when no human is looking. — [Booth, Valve slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Two concurrent behaviour systems: Main (decisions, attention, targets) and Legs, "responsible for staying near team unless otherwise directed". — [Booth, Valve slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

#### Enemy squads that seem to reason (F.E.A.R.)
- "Vocalizing intentions can sometimes even be enough, without any actual implementation of the associated squad behavior" (the "I need reinforcements" line). All decisions about what to say "are made after the fact", yet a player wrote that the soldiers "actually DO what they're told". — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- "We also use dialogue to explain a lack of action. ... The A.I. says 'I've got nowhere to go!'" — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Fire permission inferred from the player's own behaviour" is the documented equivalent of understanding "Shoot, shoot, shoot": the player's urgency and the target they are themselves engaging or pointing at carry the intent; the words only confirm it.
- A bark is a commitment. If the bot says "I'm out of ammo!", the player's next line will be about ammo. Ellie's verifiable-callout rule and F.E.A.R.'s after-the-fact dialogue both imply that anything the bot says should be backed by state that later orders can refer to.
- Reporting inability in words ("I've got nowhere to go!") is treated by Orkin as an intelligence gain, not an admission of failure.
- The absolute guarantees in Left 4 Dead (never friendly fire, never Molotovs) suggest the bot's context guesses should be bounded by a short list of things it will never do on a guess, for example throw a frag at an inferred place.
- Proactive behaviours and chatter need long cooldowns; the same complaint reappears in 2026 for PUBG Ally (section 5).

### Gaps
- The Elizabeth and Atreus talks are behind the GDC Vault paywall; only an abstract and press write-ups were read.
- No published talk was found on BT-7274 (Titanfall 2), Half-Life: Alyx's Russell, Half-Life 2's Alyx, or D-Dog and Quiet (Metal Gear Solid V). Wikipedia's MGSV page gives no buddy command details. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Gear_Solid_V:_The_Phantom_Pain)
- I found no published design rule on "remembering the last instruction" for classic companions; the evidence for it comes from the language-model systems in section 5.

## 5. Language-model companions, 2024 to October 2026

### Takeaway
The two systems with real technical documentation, KRAFTON's PUBG Ally and Tencent's F.A.C.U.L., both keep a deterministic behaviour tree in charge of moment-to-moment play and use the language model only to choose among a fixed set of high-level actions. PUBG Ally's public beta (June 2026) scored below the midpoint for command following and response speed despite a 1.6-second on-device voice exchange, and half of surveyed players saw it as a tool; F.A.C.U.L. routes most commands through a BERT-class classifier and uses a 1.5B model only as fallback.

### Cited Findings

#### PUBG Ally (KRAFTON with NVIDIA ACE) — shipped as a time-limited public beta
- Timeline: announced as a "Co-Playable Character" at CES 2025, at that point described with the 8B Mistral-Nemo-Minitron model; public beta "Ally Duo" in PUBG: BATTLEGROUNDS Arcade from 17 June 2026 for two weeks (end date given as 30 June by NVIDIA and 1 July by KRAFTON), Sanhok map only, voice in English, Korean and Chinese. — [NVIDIA, 6 Jan 2025](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-autonomous-ai-companions-pubg-naraka-bladepoint/); [KRAFTON press release, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/krafton-introduces-pubg-ally-beta-test); [NVIDIA, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-in/geforce/news/pubg-ally-ai-teammate-beta-available-now/)
- Technical report (55 pages, arXiv, 24 September 2026): a language-model agent inspects game state, interprets player speech, keeps context and decides what to say; it "issues high-level action choices" to a faster control layer for movement, combat and recovery. — [PUBG Ally Team, arXiv 2609.29837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29837)
- Split of work (developer): the behaviour tree ("System 1") handles movement, aiming and immediate combat at game tick rate without waiting for language; the model ("System 2") handles intent, coordination and speech. Defining the boundary is described as a major engineering effort. — [NVIDIA Technical Blog, 25 Jun 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)
- The control layer is a deterministic behaviour tree with a single reactive selector at the root, re-evaluated every tick. The model's commands are injected into a "direct command" branch that sits below a survival reflex. Longer-lived preferences are stored as mode variables that gate whole branches (combat engagement, looting, movement destination). A dispatched action persists until it succeeds, fails or is pre-empted. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- Models: on-device speech-to-text (NVIDIA Parakeet, running on the CPU per the report), a roughly 2B-parameter small language model per language (English Mistral-NeMo-Minitron-2B-128K-Instruct, Korean kanana-1.5-2.1b, Chinese Qwen3-1.7B), and KRAFTON's own text-to-speech. 4-bit weights are 1.0 to 1.4 GB, 1.6 to 2.1 GB with the key-value cache, inside a shared 8 GB consumer GPU target. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [NVIDIA, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-in/geforce/news/pubg-ally-ai-teammate-beta-available-now/)
- Interface: 16 callable tools in six groups (match observation 7, knowledge and memory 2, action execution 2, player communication 3, loop control 1, safety 1). Named examples: `get_combat_info()` returning text such as "Two enemies are 40 m east.", `check_action_availability(move_to)`, `execute_action(move_to, ...)`, `speak("Need cover.")`, `lookup_game_knowledge("M416")`, `compact(plan=...)`. Observation results are plain-text descriptions of engine data. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [NVIDIA Technical Blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)
- Context: a runtime budget of "roughly 5,000 tokens", ordered system prompt, tool definitions, event history, carried-over plan, current inputs, with the stable part first for cache reuse (90.7% prefix reuse per session). Each loop ends with `compact(plan=...)`; the plan keeps decisions, pending tasks, promises to the team mate and unresolved events. Match facts are not carried forward; the model must re-query them. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- Latency on RTX 4060-class GPUs: speech-to-text about 0.12 s, model inference median 0.80 s (p90 0.91 s), text-to-speech about 0.70 s, about 1.6 s for one spoken exchange on-device against 3.4 s with a cloud model. Each extra model inference adds about 0.78 s; six inferences stay under 5 s. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [Inven Global, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- Training: a base 2B model scored 0.540 on the command criterion and 0.431 on trajectory; after distillation from a Gemma 4 31B teacher it scored 0.876 and 0.902 (LLM-judged, 0 to 1). Data came from 1,046 paid players, 38,956 sessions over 28 days in a Korean PC cafe; 464K initial examples plus 313K correction examples. Scope was restricted to one map, one mode and a fixed item taxonomy. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [NVIDIA Technical Blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)
- Input is push-to-talk and turn-based: Ally cannot process a new utterance while executing tool calls. A full-duplex voice model is named as the next step. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- Player results from the live beta survey (141 countries): net recommendation +25.1 percentage points, but gameplay ratings out of 5 were combat skill 2.58, situation reading 2.88, response speed 2.93, command following 2.99. Framing: tool 50.0%, companion 31.5%, team mate 18.5%. Most liked: information and tactics 51%, someone to talk to 28%. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- Reported failure modes: acknowledging without acting ("Got it, I'll attack"), replying only "okay", dying to the blue zone because the player forgot to ask it to heal, talking too much late in a match and masking footsteps, revive hesitation, inconsistent cross-match memory. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- Speed mattered more than model quality in a blind test: the first on-device model was preferred to the cloud model by 69.5% to 18.8% (n = 154) while cloud latency was degraded; at matched latency the cloud model was preferred 52.4% to 31.1%. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- Median cloud cost per match would have been 3.70 to 7.10 US dollars with frontier models and 0.02 to 0.06 with Gemma 4 31B, against zero to the company on-device. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- Marketing wording without numbers: the voice model "synthesizes natural-sounding voice responses instantly"; "designed to react with minimal latency". — [NVIDIA, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-in/geforce/news/pubg-ally-ai-teammate-beta-available-now/); [KRAFTON press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/krafton-introduces-pubg-ally-beta-test)

#### F.A.C.U.L. in Arena Breakout: Infinite (Tencent, MoreFun Studios) — research system with a user study; public availability unclear
- Shown at Gamescom 2024; paper on arXiv November 2025 and in AAAI proceedings March 2026. The demo page says "Deployed in the popular FPS game Arena Breakout: Infinite"; Tencent's April 2025 article gives no release date. — [arXiv 2511.13112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13112v2); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38842); [demo page](https://sites.google.com/view/FACUL-demo); [Tencent, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.tencent.com/en-us/articles/2202068.html)
- Pipeline: speech recognition, then "BERT-FID", a BERT-base model (the paper lists the original BERT at 104M parameters) trained jointly on command segmentation, named entity recognition with six entity types, and a three-level hierarchical intent classifier with 25 categories. If its confidence is below a threshold the command goes to a fine-tuned Qwen2-1.5B, which writes Python that calls atomic functions (move, attack, open_door) implemented as behaviour trees. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Of 2,550 test decisions, 2,148 were handled by BERT-FID and 402 by the language model. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Unsupported requests: training adds an entropy term to lower confidence on unsupported intents; commands that need a function the behaviour trees lack (a countdown) are refused. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Grounding places: level assets carry coordinates, bounding boxes and cover points; a label-embedding search runs first, a fine-tuned CLIP image search is the fallback, and candidates are re-ranked by the player's position and facing. Hierarchical references are resolved outside-in (the motel's first floor, then the red sofa). — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Accuracy: instruction reasoning 0.714 (BERT), 0.749 (BERT-FID), 0.908 (fine-tuned 1.5B model alone), 0.886 (cascade). End to end: 87.2% for the full system, 73.9% for BERT-FID with text retrieval only, 89.2% for the language model with CLIP. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Latency about 613 ms on average for the full system, measured on server GPUs (two RTX 3080 for reasoning, two Tesla T4 for retrieval); the paper refers to "the server side". The demo page states 800 ms, 89% and a 75% cost reduction, which differ from the paper's 613 ms, 87.2% and 77%. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2); [demo page](https://sites.google.com/view/FACUL-demo)
- Context handling shown by example only: "Go to the place I just asked you to go" resolves to the earlier place; "that tree" and "Everyone else, do the same" are handled. No dialogue-history mechanism is described and corrections are not covered. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Known failures: slang ("the richest building") and fine 3D references ("the second window from the left on the second floor"). — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- User study with 24 players: F.A.C.U.L. and random human team mates were preferred for enjoyment; the command wheel ranked lowest on most aspects (rankings only, no scores). — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- Marketing: "can identify and distinguish over 17,000 in-game objects" (Tencent article, consistent with the demo page but not broken down); claims that it "learns from your gameplay" and reads emotional state from tone appear only in promotional coverage. — [Tencent](https://www.tencent.com/en-us/articles/2202068.html); [TapTap](https://www.taptap.io/ru/post/8017463)

### Inferences
- Both documented systems arrive at the project's existing architecture from the other side: a deterministic executor with standing modes, plus a component that picks one high-level action. What the language model adds is slot resolution from context, multi-step requests and speech, and the measured price is 0.8 s per inference on a mid-range GPU plus 1.6 to 2.1 GB of VRAM.
- F.A.C.U.L. is the closest published analogue of the project's classifier (encoder with intent, entities and segmentation). Its ablation gives a rough size for the gap: 73.9% end to end without the language model against 87.2% with it, on commands that include free place descriptions. The project's pin and crosshair remove much of the place-description problem that the language model and CLIP retrieval solve there.
- PUBG Ally's below-midpoint score for command following, with a distilled model and 16 tools, shows that a language model in the loop does not by itself deliver "understands what I mean". The reported failures are harness failures (said yes, did nothing; no self-preservation without being asked), which are fixable deterministically.
- The preference flip in the blind test (69.5% for the faster, weaker model; reversed at equal latency) implies that at the project's latency of a few hundred milliseconds, responsiveness is an asset worth more than a marginal gain in understanding.
- Three items of PUBG Ally's design transfer directly without a model: (1) check availability before acknowledging, (2) keep a short carried-over plan that stores pending tasks and promises, (3) store long-lived preferences as mode variables separate from the one-shot direct command, below a survival reflex.
- Restricting scope to one map with a fixed vocabulary of landmarks was necessary even for a 31B teacher. A per-map landmark list is a cheap grounding source for a classifier as well.

## 6. The smallest reported "reasoning" component

### Takeaway
The smallest documented component that turns a spoken command into a game action is not a language model: F.A.C.U.L.'s BERT-base classifier (about 104M parameters, 25 intents, six entity types) handles 84% of commands, with a 1.5B model as fallback. Where a language model is the planner, the smallest shipped one for a commanded team mate is PUBG Ally's 1.7 to 2.1B model with 16 tools and about 5,000 tokens of context; inZOI's 0.5B model is smaller but does not take squad commands.

### Cited Findings
- F.A.C.U.L.: BERT-FID decided 2,148 of 2,550 commands; the fallback is a fine-tuned Qwen2-1.5B that emits Python calling atomic behaviour-tree functions; the number of primitives is not stated. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- F.A.C.U.L. throughput on server GPUs: the BERT path about 412 ms at 1,672 queries per second, the fine-tuned 1.5B model 392 ms at 245 queries per second, the cascade 470 ms at 813. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- PUBG Ally: a language model emitting function calls to a fixed set of 16 tools, two of which execute actions (`check_action_availability`, `execute_action`); about 5,000 tokens of context; one inference at a time; 0.80 s median per inference; 1.6 to 2.1 GB VRAM at 4-bit. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- An undistilled 2B model is not usable for this: command score 0.540 before distillation, 0.876 after, using 464K plus 313K training examples from about 39,000 recorded sessions. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- PUBG Ally's published prompt structure: system prompt, tool definitions, event history, carried-over plan, current turn; tool results are plain sentences such as "You are armed with M416, 24/30 bullets in the magazine. HP 78%, inside the safe zone." — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2); [NVIDIA Technical Blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)
- inZOI Smart Zoi: 0.5B parameters on-device, selecting daily activities, with free-form text influence. — [NVIDIA, 13 Mar 2025](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-naraka-bladepoint-inzoi-launch-this-month)
- NVIDIA's smallest offered cognition model in January 2025, Mistral-Nemo-Minitron 2B, "fits in as little as 1.5GB of VRAM"; the same announcement showed PUBG Ally on the 8B model, and the shipped beta used the 2B class. — [NVIDIA, 6 Jan 2025](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-autonomous-ai-companions-pubg-naraka-bladepoint/); [NVIDIA, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-in/geforce/news/pubg-ally-ai-teammate-beta-available-now/)
- Classic systems as a baseline for "small": F.E.A.R. plans are typically one or two actions over a fixed array of world-state values; Killzone 2 averages 15 decompositions per plan. — [Thompson, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-the-ai-of-f-e-a-r-with-goal-oriented-action-planning); [Guerrilla Games slides](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots)

### Inferences
- Yes, the language-model systems are planners that emit calls to a fixed action set. The action vocabulary they finally reach (move to, follow, hold, attack, revive, loot, speak) is smaller than the project's 41 intents. Their advantage lies in argument resolution and in keeping a plan across turns, not in a richer set of actions.
- The context that carries the "understanding" is small and structured: recent events, one short plan line, the current utterance, and observations fetched on demand. That is a few dozen facts, which a rule table or a small planner can hold without a model.
- A component of about 100M parameters plus deterministic context rules is a documented, shipped-in-research configuration (F.A.C.U.L.'s BERT path). The project's classifier is in that class and already runs on the CPU.
- None of the systems runs its language model on the CPU. PUBG Ally runs only speech-to-text there. A CPU-only budget of a few hundred milliseconds excludes the 2B class as measured (0.8 s on an RTX 4060-class GPU).

### Gaps
- No published full system prompt, JSON schema or token breakdown by section was found for any shipped game companion. PUBG Ally's report gives the order and the total budget only.
- No CPU-only latency figures were found for any language-model game companion.
- Model sizes and action interfaces for NARAKA: BLADEPOINT's team mate and Ubisoft Teammates are unpublished.

## 7. Failure handling and feedback: telling the player what was understood and what cannot be done

### Takeaway
The documented practice is to make the resolved order visible or audible before or as it executes, to acknowledge only what will really happen, to give inability a spoken reason, and to keep a non-voice fallback. The newest data point (PUBG Ally, 2026) shows that a bare "okay" with no action is the failure players report first, and KRAFTON answered it with an availability check before acting and an automatic speech-action alignment check.

### Cited Findings
- PUBG Ally players reported "Got it, I'll attack" with no action and replies of only "okay". KRAFTON added a speech-action alignment grader to its offline evaluation. The agent checks availability before dispatching, sees action outcomes (completed, failed, aborted) as events on the next turn, and when an action is unavailable may propose an executable alternative such as following the player, moving to a landmark or using a ping. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- PUBG Ally's gate also fails the agent when it "replies in free text instead of calling speak" or speaks without calling `execute_action`; speech can be delayed until the current utterance finishes, and an action that conflicts with a running one is not dispatched. — [arXiv 2609.29837 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29837v2)
- Over-talking is a failure in a shooter: Ally "kept talking during late-game combat when players needed to listen for footsteps". — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- F.A.C.U.L. answers with short acknowledgements ("Copy that", "Roger that"), gives information answers, and refuses with a reason ("Negative. No friendly fire!"); unsupported behaviours are refused rather than approximated. Training data came from players' thumbs-up and thumbs-down on each response, with annotators correcting failed cases. — [arXiv 2511.13112 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13112v2)
- F.E.A.R.: speech explains inaction ("I've got nowhere to go!") and makes coordination legible; "There is no point in spending time and effort implementing squad behaviors if in the end the coordination of the A.I. is not apparent to the player." Hooking lines by hand needed care: asking "What's your status?" of a soldier whose limbs were just blown off is the kind of mismatch they had to remove. — [Orkin 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)
- Republic Commando: situations are named cues shared by programmers and sound designers, including responses to player commands (about 50 cues initially). — [GameSpot designer diary #4](https://www.gamespot.com/pc/action/starwarsrepubliccommando/preview_6117992.html)
- Visual confirmation of the resolved order: Republic Commando's visor hologram, Full Spectrum Warrior's cursor that changes from wall to corner stacking, SWAT 4's cursor icon that changes on the door. — [FutureFive](https://futurefive.co.nz/story/star-wars-republic-commando); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/full-spectrum-warrior-impressions/1100-6028426/); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/swat-4-final-hands-on/1100-6121046)
- Visual confirmation of the effect: Brothers in Arms' suppression circles; Elizabeth's spotlight icon, added because spoken callouts alone were missed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_in_Arms:_Road_to_Hill_30); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-soccer-and-theater-helped-irrational-create-bioshock-infinites-elizabeth/)
- Audio-only squad feedback draws complaints: a Rainbow Six: Vegas player review says in busy firefights "I often can't sort out what the hell is going on". — [Obsidian forum blog](https://forums.obsidian.net/blogs/entry/50-rainbow-six-vegas-review)
- Showing what can be said: EndWar lists the commands that apply to the current situation while the trigger is held; SOCOM offers its orders in an on-screen menu as well as by voice. — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/47087/tom-clancys-endwar-first-look); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCOM_U.S._Navy_SEALs_(video_game))
- False triggers and ignored commands are what reviewers punish hardest (Binary Domain), and a fallback that is always in the hands is what they praise (Mass Effect 3 with Kinect). — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/binary-domain-review); [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/3054-kinect-effect-commanding-shepards-squad-with-voice)
- Upward reporting is built into layered AI: Killzone 2 squads report "Order failed" to the strategy layer and individuals can request reassignment. — [Guerrilla Games slides](https://www.slideshare.net/guerrillagames/killzone-2-multiplayer-bots)
- Trust depends on predictability and on never calling out what the player cannot check. — [Booth, Valve slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf); [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 35](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter35_Ellie_Buddy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)

### Inferences

Design rules a small team can apply without a language model, each traced to the findings above:

1. **Resolve the place from pointing, never from the sentence alone.** Precedence for a missing place: a place named in the utterance, then a deictic word plus the crosshair hit at the time of speaking, then an active pin (with an age limit), then the place of the last order, then the bot's current target. Source pattern: Republic Commando, SWAT 4, Brothers in Arms, and the who-what-where definition that allows unstated slots.
2. **Let the target class choose or refine the verb.** "Shoot" on an enemy is attack; on a pinned spot it is fire or suppress at that spot; "check" on a door is a different action from "check" on a corner. Source pattern: SWAT 4 smart commands, Republic Commando.
3. **Keep one order of memory with a time stamp and an outcome.** "Again", "no", "here", "there", "go" and "yes" are operators on that record: repeat it, cancel and re-issue with a new place, release a held order, accept a proposal. Source pattern: F.A.C.U.L.'s "the place I just asked you to go", PUBG Ally's carried-over plan, Halo 2's short-lived stimulus behaviours.
4. **Write a rule table sorted by specificity with a general fallback for every intent.** Facts in, one resolved order out, most specific match wins. Source pattern: Valve's dynamic dialogue rules.
5. **Every bark records a fact the next order can bind to.** "I'm out of ammo!" sets a pending need; "go", "yes" or "go find ammo" then resolves to the resupply plan. Source pattern: Orkin's dialogue-as-action, PUBG Ally keeping promises in the plan, Ellie's verifiable callouts.
6. **Give the bot a small need-driven planner for self-maintenance.** Preconditions such as "has ammo" chain to a short plan (go to source, pick up, return, resume the previous order). Announce it; let a standing order veto it. Source pattern: F.E.A.R. goals and one-to-two-step plans, PUBG Ally's survival reflex above the direct-command branch and its "died because nobody asked it to heal" failure.
7. **Separate one-shot orders from standing modes.** A new point order replaces the old point order and leaves fire discipline, stance and follow mode untouched; a correction replaces only the corrected slot. Source pattern: Halo 2 orders and styles, Rainbow Six: Vegas modes, PUBG Ally mode variables.
8. **Acknowledge with the resolved content, and only after the action is known to be possible.** "Firing on your pin" rather than "okay". Source pattern: PUBG Ally's availability check and alignment grader.
9. **Say why not, and offer the nearest thing that is possible.** "No line of fire on the pin, moving left" or "Can't, no ammo". Source pattern: F.E.A.R., F.A.C.U.L. refusals, PUBG Ally alternatives.
10. **Bound guesses by a never-on-a-guess list.** Irreversible or dangerous actions (frag, leaving cover under fire, breaking a hold) need an explicit order. Source pattern: Left 4 Dead guarantees, Ellie's "never break stealth".
11. **Put proactive speech and help behind long cooldowns, and stay quiet in close combat.** Source pattern: Ellie's timers, PUBG Ally's over-talking complaint.
12. **Keep utterances short and distinct, and keep a button for the most common orders.** Source pattern: EndWar's word tuning, Rainbow Six 3's frag / flash confusion, Mass Effect 3 and SOCOM fallbacks, the attention cost of the situated commander.
13. **Prefer speed to cleverness.** A resolved order in a few hundred milliseconds with a correct echo is, on the evidence of PUBG Ally's blind test, worth more to players than a slower, better-informed one.

Mapping to the owner's five test cases (inference from the rules above): "Shoot, shoot, shoot" with a pin → rules 1 and 2. "Shoot here" → rule 1 (deictic plus crosshair). "No, here, on my pin." → rule 3 (cancel and re-issue with new place) and rule 7. "Shoot again" → rule 3. "Go find ammo." after "I'm out of ammo!" → rules 5 and 6.
